# Trademark shortlist: preliminary review

Research date: 3 October 2026. Proposed markets: Australia and United States, pending confirmation. Names: RippleTracker, Docketide, Tellwake, Filewake, CivicWake.

Status: **No name has formal legal clearance.** This is an initial screening and an attorney briefing, not a legal opinion or a comprehensive similarity search. Findings reflect the register and public pages observed on the research date.

## Product being named

The existing Ripple project is an iOS/Android records and research app. It combines congressional stock disclosures, corporate insider Form 4 filings, and White House executive/policy events, with watchlists, a timeline, source links, and stock-price context. The intended product reads and presents records; it does not execute trades or hold funds. Source: project README and store-compliance documentation.

The owner reports owning rippletracker.app. Ownership and acquisition date have not been independently verified. No commercial first-use date, applicant entity, final logo, or intended expansion into financial services has been confirmed.

The broader US RIPPLE search returned 599 records; only selected relevant entries were inspected. A RIPPLE class-9 record, serial 86052959, appeared dead/cancelled, and RIPPLE-TRAC, serial 77729098, appeared dead/abandoned. Those statuses do not establish that current common-law use has ended. This review does not rely on those dead records as active registrations.

The app's informational purpose differs from payment or currency trading services, which is a potentially helpful distinction. However, RIPPLE remains a recognizable component of RippleTracker, Tracker describes tracking, and the app operates in financial information. The class-38 specifications deserve particular scrutiny. Classification alone neither proves nor eliminates a conflict; the actual goods/services and overall commercial impression matter.

[IP Australia's comparison guidance](https://manuals.ipaustralia.gov.au/trademark/6.-factors-to-consider-when-comparing-trade-marks) explains why adding a weakly distinctive element may leave marks deceptively similar. Applying that principle to RippleTracker requires counsel's assessment, not an automatic rejection based solely on a shared word.

## What remains before formal clearance

Counsel should search similar spellings, sound-alikes, word components, pending applications, logos, and unregistered use for the shortlisted name; inspect relevant records and prosecution histories; and assess both use/infringement risk and registration prospects. US state/business registers, TMOG, WIPO/Madrid, comprehensive company-name checks, all app stores, social handles, and other launch territories have not been fully searched. No comprehensive domain-availability conclusion is made here.

Potential filing scope to discuss: downloadable mobile software (class 9), hosted software if actually offered (class 42), and any financial-information or publishing services based on the product actually delivered. These are planning topics, not final goods/services wording or a filing instruction.

The [USPTO's clearance guidance](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/search/comprehensive-clearance-search-similar-trademarks) specifically includes similar marks and common-law use; an empty exact-name federal search is insufficient.
